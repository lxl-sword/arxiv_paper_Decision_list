Spatial Foundry: A Multimodal Dataset for Spatial Intelligence

Authors:Ethan Lin, Yang Song
Date:2026-11-12 18:58:36

Spatial intelligence requires connecting visual observations to the geometry, objects, and persistent state of an environment. Images alone leave these relationships implicit, while disconnected annotations make it difficult to study appearance, camera motion, and scene changes under a common reference. We introduce Spatial Foundry, a collection of 5,280 furnished residential 3D scene configurations with editable assets and aligned visual, geometric, and temporal data. Its modalities include floor plans, perspective and panoramic RGB, metric depth, point clouds, meshes, instance-level 3D bounding boxes, and camera trajectories paired with video. Shared scene coordinates link observations across views and time; editable source scenes enable additional camera paths and controlled object changes. We describe the data representation and construction workflow, and discuss applications in floor-plan-conditioned 3D generation, geometric reconstruction, instance-level perception, camera-conditioned video generation, shared world modeling, and scene editing. Controlled camera and scene variations support spatial perception and world-model research. The public project page provides videos and an interactive colored point-cloud preview.

UAV-Flow++: A Unified Suite for Benchmarking and Learning Language-Guided UAV Flight

Date:2026-11-12 17:39:43

Language-guided UAV navigation has largely focused on long-horizon route planning, leaving short-range, object-relative flight maneuvers underexplored. To address this gap, we formulate Flying-on-a-Word (Flow), a task requiring a UAV to convert concise natural-language instruction, egocentric visual observation, and current state dynamics into continuous flight actions. Flow encompasses both primitive motion commands and complex object-interactive behaviors, assessing motion intent comprehension and spatial context grounding. To enable policy learning and closed-loop evaluation, we introduce UAV-Flow, a comprehensive dataset comprising 30,692 expert-piloted real-world trajectories across eight distinct motion categories, alongside 10,109 simulation trajectories in the UAV-Flow-Sim environment equipped with task-specific completion criteria. Physical UAV experiments demonstrate robust execution of object-relative commands in both indoor and outdoor real-world scenes. Together, Flow establishes a novel benchmark and learning framework for language-conditioned flight maneuvers.

Certified Hierarchical Planning and Tracking: A Learning-Based Approach

Authors:Weijian Li, Vince Kurtz, Panos J. Antsaklis, Hai Lin
Date:2026-11-12 15:49:24

In this paper, we develop a hierarchical control framework for nonlinear dynamical systems with safety constraints. Specifically, a low-fidelity model is introduced for trajectory planning, while a tracking controller is designed for the high-fidelity system. We first characterize the tracking error bound (TEB) using a storage function. Then, we formulate the synthesis of the tracking controller, the storage function, and the TEB as an optimization problem. Following that, we develop an offline learning approach to approximately solve the optimization problem, by incorporating behavior cloning and satisfiability modulo theories (SMT) solver. We further integrate the learned controller and the TEB into online tracking of the high-fidelity system. This framework enables the control of high-dimensional nonlinear systems with safety guarantees, while maintaining computational efficiency for real-time implementation. Finally, experiments are provided for illustration.

CT-SGAL-LIO: Causal Continuous Time Refinement of Discrete LiDAR Inertial Odometry

Authors:Nam Tran, Thu Tran, Toan Nguyen, William J Beksi, Tuan Dang
Date:2026-11-12 15:07:17

State-of-the-art LiDAR-inertial odometry methods, including those on richer state manifolds, achieve strong global accuracy, yet their pose stream alternates between propagation and correction and can oscillate over short horizons even when globally accurate. Most existing methods follow these approaches: they either modify the filter to enable faster updates or estimate a continuous-time trajectory from raw LiDAR and inertial data. The latter involves extra optimization, delaying state updates and becoming less robust during rapid rotations or accelerations. We propose CT-SGAL-LIO, a continuous-time refinement module that smooths the posterior pose stream of a discrete SGal(3) estimator without modifying the estimator itself. The key novelty is to perform continuous-time trajectory refinement directly on the projected SGal(3) posterior: we fit a cubic trajectory over a bounded window of recent poses and evaluate it at a small fixed lag. This places the query time within the observed interval, so the refined pose is obtained by interpolation rather than extrapolation, while preserving causal operation. CT-SGAL-LIO improves relative trajectory accuracy on 17 sequences from three public benchmarks. Across 17 sequences from GRAND-TOUR, MCD, and GEODE, CT-SGAL-LIO improves short-term motion accuracy while largely preserving global accuracy. RTE improves by up to 70% on GRAND-TOUR and on all seven GEODE sequences, with 9.9-51.1% reductions. A 19.5-min R-Campus run also reduces posterior jumps by 85.4% with only a 4.7 mm change in the end-to-start gap. We plan to release our source code and results to support community validation and reproducibility.

Informative Path Planning with Gaussian Processes: Error Bounds, Algorithms, and Benchmarks

Authors:Kalvik Jakkala, Jennifer Wakulicz, Donald G. Dansereau, Srinivas Akella, Lantao Liu, Jason M. O'Kane
Date:2026-11-12 14:41:08

Accurate environmental monitoring relies on Informative Path Planning (IPP) to deploy autonomous systems that maximize information gain. While Gaussian Processes (GPs) provide a robust mathematical foundation for modeling the environment, contemporary IPP approaches remain hindered by heuristic operational constraints and the lack of simultaneous theoretical guarantees for worst-case estimation error and approximation quality. Rather than introducing yet another heuristic, this paper presents a rigorous theoretical analysis of existing error bounds and algorithms to further develop the mathematical foundation for IPP. By evaluating probabilistic and deterministic bounds on reconstruction error, we establish that predictive variance is the theoretically ideal optimization objective for GP-based IPP. Furthermore, we analyze GreedyCover, a recent approach for optimizing predictive variance, and demonstrate that its near-optimal approximation guarantee matches the fundamental hardness-of-approximation limit for the problem. Comprehensive benchmarking confirms that this approach delivers superior estimation accuracy and computational efficiency compared to prominent information-theoretic objectives and optimization methods used in IPP. Finally, this theoretical analysis establishes a rigorous, near-optimal foundation for discrete offline IPP under bounded uncertainty constraints, offering clear and principled guidance for future work in continuous-space and online formulations.

TRACE: Target-Trial Relevance Assessment and Compatibility-Guided Evidence Synthesis

Authors:Wei Wei, Liangze Ke, Tianzhi Tang, Pavan Challa, Tulika Rudra Gupta, Guannan Gong, Satrajit Roychoudhury, Raghav Sundar
Date:2026-11-12 14:38:17

External trial evidence can inform oncology trial design, but broad pooling may produce benchmarks poorly aligned with the planned population. We developed TRACE (Target-Trial Relevance Assessment and Compatibility-Guided Evidence Synthesis), combining eligibility screening, Bayesian compatibility assessment using published baseline characteristics, and hierarchical survival modeling. An integrated compatibility score based on the largest standardized difference in jointly reported characteristics identifies compatible external trials. Their reconstructed control-arm survival data are synthesized using a hierarchical beta-Stacy process to generate a predictive survival benchmark. We evaluated TRACE using eleven randomized phase III trials in first-line HER2-negative advanced gastric/gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma. With KEYNOTE-859 as the target, three external trials were selected. TRACE predicted a control-arm median overall survival of 11.8 months (95% posterior predictive interval, 8.9-15.2), compared with an observed median of 11.8 months (95% confidence interval, 10.9-12.7). In leave-one-trial-out validation, seven targets had compatible external evidence. Predictive intervals contained the observed median for all seven. In comparison, confidence intervals from Kaplan-Meier estimates pooling all other trials' control arms contained it for five. Mean pointwise curve coverage was 92% for TRACE and 59% for pooled Kaplan-Meier. For four targets without compatible external evidence, TRACE returned a weakly informative prior predictive benchmark. TRACE provides a transparent approach to selecting relevant external evidence and generating benchmarks for oncology trial planning.

Data-Driven Robust Inventory Planning and Advertising under Product Return

Authors:Hongyan Zhang, Yugang Yu, Qinshen Tang, Ye Shi, Li Chen
Date:2026-11-12 13:20:36

Online advertising is a central marketing lever for e-commerce retailers, yet it complicates operational planning by simultaneously stimulating product demand and increasing product returns. Motivated by the practice of a fashion retailer on Amazon, we study a retailer's joint inventory and advertising problem across multiple products over a single season, in which advertising has both direct and cross-product spillover effects on demand and returns. Using linear net-demand and return models that capture both effects, we formulate a contextual stochastic optimization model that jointly determines inventory levels and advertising decisions before the selling season. Because ad clicks are realized only after the advertising decision, the predictive covariates are partially observable and decision-dependent, and their joint distribution with the demand and return errors is unknown. We therefore develop an estimate-then-robustify (ETR) approach grounded in a data-driven robust satisficing framework. On the retailer's data, ETR improves out-of-sample profit over conventional estimate-then-optimize by 6.39\% and earns the higher profit in 74.5% of instances, with the largest gains when training data are scarce; the gain falls to 1.02% when the decision model omits cross-product spillovers.

Authors:Cara Koepele, Kevin Wallington, Georgios Mavromatidis, John Lygeros
Date:2026-11-12 12:16:30

The value of renewable energy devices depends on their operation, which must adapt to weather and demand using limited, imperfect forecasts that update over time. Conventional energy systems planning practices assume unlimited foresight in a single-level formulation, potentially leading to long-term investment in suboptimal device sizes. Accounting for realistic operation corresponds to a bi-level problem: the upper level selects device sizes, while the lower level contains closed-loop operation under evolving forecasts. We solve this problem using backpropagation MPC (BP-MPC), a gradient-based method, with a deterministic MPC as the lower-level controller. Using forecast errors generated by a parameterized martingale model of forecast evolution, we investigate how forecast accuracy affects the benefits of operation-aware energy systems planning and the resulting device sizes. NSDL: Neuro-Symbolic Division of Labor for Multi-Robot Task Planning

Authors:Xincheng Tan, Ke Wang, Yining Di, Minfeng Zhu
Date:2026-11-12 11:47:48

Planning for a heterogeneous robot team from a scene image and an instruction involves two kinds of decisions. Some must be judged from the scene: which goals are already met, how the task splits, and which robot does what. Others follow from these and can be computed: prerequisite actions, handoffs between robots, and timing. We argue that each kind should go to the component suited to it: a small vision-language model learns the first, and a deterministic solver computes the second. In NSDL (Neuro-Symbolic Division of Labor), the trained model writes its judgments as a plan sketch, which lists the goals that still need action and the intended steps in order, each with a robot. The solver expands the sketch into an executable joint plan. The model calls two tools, a detector for evidence and the solver, and is trained with the solver in the loop. We evaluate on the public VIKI-L2 benchmark and on LOCUS-Bench, a new benchmark that grades the two kinds of difficulty separately. NSDL outperforms end-to-end planners and formalize-then-plan baselines on both benchmarks and with three backbones from 3B to 8B; the 7B model reaches 97.0% out-of-distribution accuracy on VIKI-L2. Ordering and scheduling errors nearly disappear once the solver writes the structure; the remaining failures are misjudged states, and most goals the model misses are ones it never lists. Code, model weights, and LOCUS-Bench are publicly available at https://github.com/Xincheng-Tan/NSDL.

Actions in expert demonstrations exhibit temporal structure. An ordered-versus-shuffled analysis across five environments confirms this dependence. We ask whether action proposals that better reflect expert continuity can improve visual world-model planning. Rather than imposing generic smoothness, we learn continuations from the temporal structure of demonstrated actions. Our locality-gated deep-kernel Gaussian-process prior, LG-DKGP, combines recent and longer-range executed action history, predicts a neural continuation, and learns a temporal Gaussian-process (GP) residual. The algorithm constructs an anytime-valid upper confidence sequence on this quantity, so the developer can repeatedly test a system, inspect the bound, and decide to stop when it falls below a chosen threshold without invalidating the statistical guarantee. Our problem can be interpreted as a sequential variant of the missing-mass estimation problem which gave rise to the classic Good-Turing estimator (Good, 1953). On SWE-Chat, a dataset of real-world coding agent traces (Baumann et al., 2026), we implement our procedure and show that it achieves tighter upper bounds than a simple multiple-testing corrected Good-Turing baseline, and even dominates a fixed-horizon (non-multiple-testing-corrected) Good-Turing upper confidence bound, which is not anytime valid. Additionally, to better match realistic evaluation pipelines, we extend our method to handle sampling from a different task distribution and imperfect automated failure labeling.

Latent Imagination for Parameterized Action Spaces

Authors:Atahan Cilan, İhsan Mert Muhacıroğlu, Ümit Can Bekar
Date:2026-11-11 17:25:22

Parameterized-action MDPs (PAMDPs) ask an agent to choose a discrete action together with continuous parameters whose meaning depends on that choice. Model-free PAMDP methods act fast but need many interactions; model-based methods are sample-efficient but plan at every decision, which makes them slow to act. We introduce HyLI (Hybrid Latent Imagination), a model-based agent that learns a hybrid policy entirely in the latent imagination of a world model and acts with a single forward pass. To address learning problems that the PAMDP structure creates, HyLI adds counterfactual mode evaluation, which gives every discrete action a learning signal at every imagined state, and success-window replay, which keeps rare successes in training. On seven standard PAMDP benchmarks, HyLI is within one published standard deviation of, or above, the published score of the strongest model-free baseline in our comparison on all 7 tasks, reaches 90% of that score with 2.4x fewer environment steps than that baseline (5.0x when its reward-free pre-training is counted), and decides 67x faster than the model-based planner DLPA at its published planner settings. It is the only method in our comparison that is both more sample-efficient than that baseline and fast enough for real-time control.

Robot-Induced Bias in Movement Smoothness Metrics for Rehabilitation: Quantification and Correction in Simulation

Authors:David Regev, Alex Scherbakov, Eran Beeri Bamani
Date:2026-11-11 17:24:43

Robot mechanics can bias kinematic measures of recovery and limit comparisons across rehabilitation devices. We developed a closed-loop human-robot simulation calibrated to published healthy and stroke group kinematics. Paired runs held motor-control parameters and movement plans fixed to quantify bias in seven metrics relative to a low-impedance reference, normalized using external repeatability benchmarks. Within a subset informed by published device specifications, median log dimensionless jerk (LDLJ) bias was 0.93 and 1.79 benchmark units for stroke and healthy profiles, respectively. Sobol analysis identified Coulomb friction and apparent mass as leading contributors; LDLJ bias persisted across 11 robustness variants. LDLJ bias increased as simulated movements became less fragmented. If recovery similarly reduces fragmentation, this effect could partly mask improvement in measured smoothness. Correction combining identified robot dynamics and observed metrics with gradient-boosted trees reduced median normalized absolute error from 0.77 to 0.10 for LDLJ and from 0.24 to 0.08 for spectral arc length (SPARC) in the device-informed subset of a random 20% hold-out. Median smoothness residuals remained at or below 0.15 benchmark units across four separately held-out parameter regions. Under an alternative controller, LDLJ error decreased from 1.60 to 0.17 without retraining, although transfer varied across metrics. These simulation results support mechanical characterization and movement-dependent correction toward more comparable robotic assessments, motivating validation in patients.
