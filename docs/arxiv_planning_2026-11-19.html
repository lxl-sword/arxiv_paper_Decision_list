Does the Action Matter? Probing Action-Conditioned Semantic Forecasting as a World Model for Autonomous Driving

Authors:Binay Kumar Singh, Niels Da Vitoria Lobo
Date:2026-11-18 18:06:20

World models are increasingly used in autonomous driving to predict how a scene evolves under the ego vehicle's actions, supporting counterfactual reasoning and model-based planning. However, existing evaluations focus on prediction fidelity and planning performance, and rarely verify that the predictions depend on the conditioning action. In this paper, we study action dependence in action-conditioned semantic forecasting, which predicts future semantic segmentation from past segmentations and the ego action. We compare a forecaster with feature-wise and input-level action conditioning against an identical model without action input, and measure action dependence with test-time action interventions scored by standard segmentation metrics. The evaluation is validated in a synthetic environment with known action-dependent dynamics and applied to Cityscapes and BDD100K, both within each dataset and across the two vehicle platforms. These results show that an accuracy gain indicates that a driving world model uses its action, but not how strongly its forecasts depend on it. Authors:Tushin Mallick, Evan Rose, Matthew Laws, Alina Oprea, Cristina Nita-Rotaru
Date:2026-11-18 18:05:47

One popular architecture for multi-agent systems (MAS) relies on an orchestrator that generates an initial plan, coordinates its execution, and dynamically revises the plan based on agent feedback when tasks cannot be fully specified in advance. This dynamic replanning introduces a critical vulnerability: malicious agents can embed instructions within their feedback to hijack the replanning process. Existing defenses focus exclusively on per-tool access control or static, pre-execution policy enforcement, leaving the replanning phase unsecured. We introduce Kaer, a multi-agent framework featuring security-aware replanning. Kaer shields the planner from untrusted agent feedback by conveying diagnostics to the replanning procedure strictly through abstract templates. Evaluating Kaer on Cintra against Magentic-One (an undefended MAS) and ControlValve (the state-of-the-art defense) demonstrates that Kaer achieves higher robustness than both systems while maintaining comparable utility to ControlValve. Although abstract failure templates slightly constrain utility on select tasks, Kaer enables MAS to perform secure, dynamic plan revisions without exposing the orchestrator to untrusted agent feedback.

Counterfactual World Models: JEPAs That Reason About "What If"

Authors:Randall Balestriero, Yann LeCun, Kerda Varaku
Date:2026-11-18 17:24:00

World models operate in a world they cannot fully observe. When a hidden disturbance, such as a current pushing a boat, changes the outcome of an action, a counterfactual question follows: what would have happened under a different action, subject to the same disturbance? Its answer separates bad decisions from bad luck. We prove that world models trained on factual transitions cannot answer it on their own, whatever their data or capacity. For many physical systems, the observed outcome and a simple property of the dynamics suffice: natura non facit saltus, nature makes no leaps. When actions and disturbances act as separate forces, their interaction is second-order in time, so the counterfactual outcome is, to first order, the observed outcome plus the difference between the world model's predictions under the alternative and factual actions. This yields a counterfactual world model with no counterfactual data, extra parameters, or retraining. For transitions of duration $h$, we prove an $O(h^2)$ bound on the counterfactual error relative to the disturbance variance, establish a matching minimax lower bound from unpaired factual data, and characterize failures at discontinuities, coordinate folds, and disturbances that scale the command. We validate these predictions from pixels with JEPA world models on frozen DINOv2 and masked autoencoder (MAE) encoders. On four rendered simulators, our approach improves counterfactual $R^2$ from $38$--$46\%$ to $86$--$96\%$ with the same world models; on a fifth, an underwater vehicle, it reaches $90$--$94\%$ with sixteen times more factual data. Four further frozen encoders, DINOv3, SigLIP~2, CLIP and EVA-02, give $81$--$95\%$ on the same simulators. In hindsight planning, it finds the action that would have reached the goal in $35$--$69\%$ of events, against $14$--$23\%$ for an oracle planner that knows the simulator but not the outcome.

4D-TEMPO: Differentiable 4D-STEM ptychography via fast Physics Optimizers

Authors:Ramani Duraiswami, Yichao Zhang
Date:2026-11-18 17:23:50

Electron ptychography recovers atomic structure from 4D-STEM data: diffraction patterns at multiple positions of a scanned probe. Current codes reconstruct an image and read atoms off it, or fit atoms under an $L_1$ loss; neither gives per-atom uncertainty. We propose 4D-TEMPO, a differentiable physical renderer that fits the atoms, the microscope, and the detector directly to the recorded electron counts under a Poisson likelihood. The output is a list of atoms, each a Gaussian primitive with a 3D position, a thermal width, and a model-conditional error bar from the Fisher information. The Fisher information also conditions the solver, gives the dose (electrons per Å$^2$) each parameter needs, and separates calibration from imaging. On a simulated WSe$_2$ bilayer, starting from the published reconstruction, fitted atoms cut in-plane error by a third (3.2 vs. 4.8 pm) and depth error by 40-61% against its 3D peaks. Depth comes from multiple scattering into the dark field, the faint counts outside the direct beam. The two layers, $10\times$ closer than the depth of field, separate; per-atom depth then follows the layer geometry. When the model is well specified, the bars are calibrated (69% of position coordinates within $1σ$). On experimental WSe$_2$, fitted atoms predict held-out patterns better than the three published codes we test. With free probe modes where atoms are incomplete, the same likelihood predicts best on all three held-out benchmarks, matches every WSe$_2$ baseline in 5 min against up to 20.5 h, and passes torchslice at its released setting 180$\times$ sooner on PrScO$_3$. Per-atom error bars, planned doses, and fast reconstruction point to low-dose, real-time 3D imaging of beam-sensitive and dynamic materials.

Attention Is Not Relevance: Challenging the Causality of Attention in Autonomous Driving

Authors:Luca Lusvarghi, Javier Gozalvez
Date:2026-11-18 08:42:22

Attention has become one of the most widely adopted solutions for estimating the relevance of surrounding objects and interpreting an autonomous vehicle's driving decisions. However, its ability to capture the causal impact of individual input elements on an AI model's output has long been discussed in the literature, particularly in the Natural Language Processing (NLP) domain. This work brings this discussion into the autonomous driving domain by analyzing the accuracy of attention in estimating the impact of an input object on an autonomous vehicle's driving, i.e., its relevance. To this end, we compare different attention-based relevance estimation methods against a causal reference of object relevance obtained through counterfactual analysis. We perform our analysis on more than 80,000 synthetic driving scenes using an autonomous vehicle based on PlanT 2.0, a state-of-the-art Transformer-based model for object-level planning. Results show that attention provides a correlational rather than causal indication of object relevance. Attention-based relevance estimation methods do not consistently identify all relevant objects and tend to incorrectly classify as relevant objects with little or no impact on the vehicle's driving. In particular, results show that attention can effectively identify relevant objects in simple single-lane, straight-road scenarios, but its accuracy degrades in more complex settings, such as multi-lane roads and intersections. These findings highlight the limitations of attention as a proxy for object relevance, calling for novel methods able to accurately capture the causal impact of individual objects on the vehicle's driving and distinguish it from correlational associations.

Agreement Is Not Accuracy: LiDAR Elevation Errors Hidden in LiDAR-Camera Extrinsics

Authors:Yeon Kun Lee, Dongseok Yang, Deok-Kyeong Jang, Choongho Chung, Junyoung Lee, Hyeongseok So
Date:2026-11-18 07:12:08

LiDAR and cameras provide complementary measurements for 3D vision, yet agreement between their observations does not establish the accuracy of either sensor's internal geometry. We show that systematic LiDAR elevation biases can be absorbed into the estimated extrinsics. Using synchronized LiDAR intensities and camera-estimated checkerboard poses, we jointly estimate a sensor-specific elevation correction shared across recordings and extrinsics for each recording. Ray-plane intersections locate the checker pattern without using reported ranges, preventing range errors from directly moving the lookup. Under matched intensity and correction models, this lookup gives smaller camera-consistency residuals than measured-point lookup. Across six identically designed rigs using the same sensor models, recording-excluded corrections reduce the mean translation-reference distance from 7.57 to 3.04 mm. On separate structures, they reduce inter-plane angle errors against references from independent dense camera scans and improve image-edge alignment. Within the ray-plane model, calibration-target projections and loop-consistency residuals change little despite these geometric improvements. These findings show why agreement after extrinsic calibration need not establish correct internal LiDAR geometry. Synchronized observations of a familiar checkerboard provide a practical route to estimating and testing elevation corrections without a dedicated large calibration field. We plan to release code and data for reproducing the main results.
