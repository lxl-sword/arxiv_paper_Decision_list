Cost-Sensitive Reversibility of Non-Deterministic Actions

Authors:Giuseppe Canonaco, Alberto Pozanco, Daniel Borrajo
Date:2026-11-23 10:02:56

Determining action reversibility requires checking whether the effects of an action can be undone. Recent work has extended reversibility to fully observable non-deterministic (FOND) planning. It distinguishes weak reversibility, where reversal may succeed, from strong-cyclic reversibility, where reversal is guaranteed under fairness. These notions are qualitative: they say whether reversal is possible, but not how costly it is. We introduce cost-sensitive reversibility for non-deterministic actions. Given positive action costs, we define weak and strong reversal budgets, $k$-bounded reversibility, and a distinction between finite strong reversal and strong-cyclic reversal with unbounded worst-case cost. We relate the resulting notions in a reversibility lattice, prove cost-aware policy-merging results, and show how to compute restricted weak and strong universal uniform reversal budgets using cost-optimal classical planning and worst-case-optimal FOND search, respectively. We also give an admissible lower-bound test for rejecting $k$-bounded reversibility. These notions support finer-grained safety decisions: a small finite strong budget bounds recovery cost, whereas a small weak budget certifies only a cheap possible recovery path. Actions without an acceptable strong bound may therefore warrant human approval, as in tool-using agent systems.
