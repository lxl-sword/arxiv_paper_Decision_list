Date:2026-10-30 17:16:59

Autonomous wheelchairs can improve the quality of life for individuals with mobility impairments by providing greater independence. However, reliable navigation especially in human environments is a major challenge due to factors such as dynamic obstacles, unpredictable pedestrian behavior, and the users' need to complete multiple stops along the way before reaching their final destination. This paper presents a hybrid path planning framework for multi-goal navigation in dynamic environments. The framework integrates the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP) for global task ordering with the Potential Field (PF) method and heuristic local planners such as Rapidly-exploring Random Tree (RRT) and A-star (A*) to achieve collision-free navigation. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed hybrid framework enables efficient multi-goal navigation while maintaining safe operation in dynamic environments with humans, with TSP-PF-RRT achieving fewer or no collisions and shorter travel distances.

This paper proposes a maximum a posteriori (MAP) inference framework with a truncated diffusion prior for safe planning (TDP-SafeMAP). This paper presents an adaptive planning approach for multi-target servicing campaigns in low Earth orbit and applies it to inspection missions flown by a low-thrust servicer. The approach is based on Distributional Reinforcement Learning, which learns the full distribution of mission returns and thereby supports explicit risk-sensitive decision-making at each planning step. Specifically, a Quantile-Regression Deep Q-Network (QR-DQN) agent selects which target to inspect next by jointly considering the expectation and the variance of the predicted return through a mean-variance criterion. The mission environment models circular orbits with a spread in inclination and uses Edelbaum's combined-manoeuvre low-thrust transfer estimates. The observation comprises the orbital elements, the visit history, the observed risk state of each target and the remaining mission-time budget. Because the mission-time budget generally admits only a subset of the candidate targets, the planner must also select which targets to inspect. Numerical experiments on scenarios with five target satellites show that one risk-aversion weight moves the policy across a range of mean return and return variance, over an unchanged environment and reward. Risk-averse agents reduce the variance of the episodic return by approximately one half relative to risk-neutral agents and lower the probability of a mission loss, at a cost in mean return. To validate the learned policies, the efficient frontier, which gives the highest mean return attainable at each level of return variance, is computed exactly by enumerating the admissible visit orders. At equal variance, the learned policies lie close to this frontier in mean return, and the risk-averse policies have a lower probability of a mission loss than the frontier policies.

Fidelity Synopses: Pre-Execution Plan Selection for Approximate Vector Search

Authors:Adithya Ramanatha, Sanskar S. Singh, Maya Ramanath
Date:2026-10-30 08:19:23

Vector search has become a database operator with many physical implementations: different index families, build-time and search-time parameters, and compressed or disk-resident variants. Each trades cost against fidelity, the fraction of a query's exact top-k nearest neighbors that a configuration returns. Deployments nevertheless choose one configuration offline and use it for every query, so they are sized for their hardest queries and overpay for the rest. On a 5.8M-item LAION benchmark with four embedding models, running each query on the cheapest configuration that suffices for it would roughly halve the cost in a typical setting. A fidelity synopsis divides the embedding space into cells around a set of landmarks and records, for every cell and configuration, how often that configuration met a fidelity target on a sample of queries. The saving persists against the best static choice among the same indexes, and within a single index at no extra memory.

Existing cross-embodiment policies output normalized commands that disregard these differences, or compensate through per-robot training and distillation. World models can in principle pool cross-embodiment data, yet command-conditioned models bind their latent dynamics to a single action space, while pose-conditioned models omit the mapping from commands to body-specific motion.
