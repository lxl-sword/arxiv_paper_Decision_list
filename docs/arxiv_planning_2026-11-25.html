Date:2026-11-24 18:57:48

Embodied Spatial Reasoning (ESR) is central to deploying Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in real-world embodied tasks, yet remains inadequately evaluated. Existing benchmarks primarily assess spatial reasoning in static images or synthetic indoor environments, where semantic shortcuts often suffice, making it difficult to faithfully evaluate spatial reasoning capabilities in the wild. In light of this, we present Lost on Campus, a benchmark for evaluating ESR in large-scale real-world outdoor 3D environments reconstructed by 3D Gaussian Splatting. Our benchmark introduces a unified reasoning-action evaluation framework that seamlessly integrates diagnostic QA for isolated reasoning with closed-loop interactive navigation for active reasoning under multimodal instructions. To enable fine-grained diagnosis, we evaluate six capability aspects of ESR: action grounding, spatial foresight, metric awareness, goal-directed planning, ego-allocentric localization, and spatio-temporal consistency. Extensive experiments reveal that: (i) Compared to indoor settings, real-world outdoor environments impose substantially higher demands on spatial reasoning, where existing models exhibit poor performance; (ii) VLMs still struggle with fine-grained visual alignment, with failures in spatial foresight and ego-allocentric localization emerging as primary bottlenecks; (iii) Improving multimodal interaction capabilities and long-term spatial reasoning is crucial for advancing embodied intelligence. These findings highlight that faithful evaluation of ESR demands benchmarks tightly coupling perception, reasoning and action within realistic and continuous environments.

Authors:Emir Cem Gezer, Ozan Kaya, Ingrid B. Utne, Roger Skjetne
Date:2026-11-24 15:01:21

Maritime Autonomous Surface Ships (MASS) must demonstrate safety before and during operations, necessitating online risk assessment to guide decision-making at sea. This paper presents a risk-informed Rapidly Exploring Random Tree Star (RRT*) path planner that integrates a Bayesian Belief Network (BBN) as an online risk model. The BBN evaluates the probability and severity distribution of hazardous events, which are aggregated into a bounded scalar risk index. During the search, this risk index is evaluated at sample points along candidate edges. The planner optimizes a composite cost function comprising path distance, heading change penalties, and accumulated risk, subject to a hard constraint enforcing a risk acceptance limit. Visual world models enable goal-directed planning by predicting action outcomes. Video generation models synthesize realistic imagery yet still struggle to produce physically plausible evolution. Existing methods guide generation with a motion trajectory predicted for the entire clip in advance, so prediction errors accumulate and degrade later frames. We propose STEP(Short-Term Event Planning), a training-free closed-loop framework that guides generation one short clip at a time. Each round consists of four steps: grounding measures the scene state from the latest frames, event planning predicts the next physical event and renders it into a control clip, latent-guided generation produces the next short clip under this control, and verification checks whether the event occurs and resamples the clip if it does not. Learning latent actions from unlabeled video sequences offers a promising way to acquire reusable action representations without relying on embodiment-specific control annotations. However, existing latent-action pretraining approaches commonly couple the modeling of visual transitions with action inference, directly mapping paired observations or derived transition cues into latent action codes. This formulation leaves two distinct tasks entangled: characterizing the relational structure of an observed transition and abstracting it into a compact action representation. We propose RELACT, a relation-first framework that explicitly separates transition relation modeling from predictive action abstraction. RELACT first constructs a structured relation representation $R$ that captures how visual elements and their interactions evolve across observations.
