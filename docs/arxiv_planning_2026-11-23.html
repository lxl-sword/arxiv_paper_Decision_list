What Must a Model Tell a Planner? Outcome Interfaces for Risk-Aware Planning After Robot Damage

Authors:Hassan Iqbal, Krishna Kumar
Date:2026-11-20 18:12:49

When a robot is damaged, it must decide which routes it can still afford, and the amount of risk it may accept can change from one mission to the next. We argue that whether a learned model helps such a robot depends on its decision interface, that is, on what the model hands to the planner. A decision policy tells the planner what to do, whereas an outcome model, a kind of world model, predicts the consequences of each route. We study simulated Unitree Go2 quadrupeds and H1 humanoids with weakened motors, added payloads, or reduced foot friction. We compare four interfaces. The first is a decision policy, and the second is the same policy trained with fixed penalties for infeasible routes. The third is an outcome model that predicts the feasibility and travel time of each route. The fourth, which we call a capability contract, is an outcome model that predicts the chance of success and the travel time of the robot on each terrain and composes them along a route. All share the same collected experience, matched tuning and risk calibration, and an uncertainty guard. Two comparatively low-risk probes let the capability contract rank hazardous terrains about as well as four new trials on every hazard. At a calibrated 5% limit on selecting empirically infeasible routes, outcome models cut declined missions from about 32% to 7-8% on Go2 and from 57% to 35-36% on H1 compared with the plain policy. Fixed penalties narrow this gap, but the best penalty changes with the risk target. Predicting outcomes lets the planner preserve missions by changing routes as risk requirements tighten.

Authors:Yun Yan, Shizhi Hu, Zhichao Li, Yang Guan, Shiqi Liu, Jiaxin Gao, Xiangteng Zhang, Wenbo Ding, Jiuhua Zhao, Hui Guo, Xintao Yan, Keqiang Li, Shengbo Eben Li
Date:2026-11-20 16:32:53

Frontier models have demonstrated remarkable general-purpose reasoning capabilities, opening new possibilities for intelligent decision making in the physical world. In autonomous driving, many recent approaches seek to leverage these capabilities through additional adaptation for action output, particularly in scenarios where existing models underperform. We introduce Generalist Action Critic (GAC), a zero-shot framework that combines frontier models' general-purpose reasoning with the planning competence of specialized driving models through an inference-time harness. In GAC, specialized planners generate multiple candidate trajectories and make an initial selection, while a frozen frontier model serves as an action critic that assesses the selected trajectory against scene evidence and either retains it or reselects from the available candidates. The harness combines camera images with spatial overlays, provides driving context and reasoning principles through textual instructions, and regulates the information available at each stage to support trajectory assessment and reselection. These results demonstrate the potential of harnessing frontier models to improve driving safety through inference-time cooperation with specialized planners.

An Autonomous Adaptive Learning System for Robotic Grasping in the Circular Factory

Authors:Yitian Shi, Rosa Wolf, Rania Rayyes
Date:2026-11-20 16:10:16

Linear manufacturing typically operates in highly structured settings with standardized objects, allowing robots to execute pre-programmed motions based on precise models. In contrast, circular manufacturing involves dynamic and less controlled environments, where product components can vary due to appearance and physical changes across different product generations. In this context, uncertainty arises from both the unknown or partially observed states of components (e.g., damaged surfaces, altered shapes) and the unstructured disassembly environment, often characterized by heavy clutter, occlusions, and limited sensing viewpoints. This demands a flexible and adaptable robotic grasping system that can cope with unexpected object changes. To address these challenges, this paper proposes an autonomous grasp learning framework designed for highly uncertain and variable conditions. The framework explicitly distinguishes between uncertainty due to noisy or incomplete sensory observations (aleatoric uncertainty) and uncertainty due to limited knowledge and distribution shift (epistemic uncertainty). Moreover, the framework employs an active perception strategy to reduce visual ambiguity: semantic and spatial scene understanding is achieved using vision language models (VLMs), while view planning enables the robot to adjust its viewpoint to improve observability. In addition, the approach leverages large-scale human demonstration data to learn interaction patterns, which are then transferred to robotic end-effectors, enabling robust manipulation in these variable settings. Quantitative validation, including the utility of active vision on disassembled components in practice, is expected to form an important part of future work.

Authors:Siân Lindley, Britta Burlin, Christian Poelitz, Lev Tankelevitch, Richard Banks
Date:2026-11-20 15:39:11

People are increasingly working with AI agents as part of extended, asynchronous workflows. In these settings, shared access to information does not guarantee a shared basis for action: goals, plans, and roles may persist while their current significance changes. We introduce coordination continuity, the extent to which collaborators sustain a workable basis for interdependent action as work progresses. Drawing on research on collaborative work, along with two qualitative team studies and a reflexive case from our own repository-based environment, we present a framework of three elements: durable representations of collaborative work; relative coordination properties that support interpretation of representations in the moment; and interaction mechanisms through which the status of representations can be understood and altered. Date:2026-11-20 15:25:07

Following harness-engineering principles, CAAF assigns task interpretation, planning, knowledge synthesis, and context management to a large language model while delegating numerical calculations to specialized tools. The results demonstrate how human-guided agentic coordination can combine engineering tools for iterative analysis and optimization with rigorous simulation-based verification under a fixed process topology.

SOAR: Training-Free Rank-Consistent Rollout Compression for Latent World Model Planning

Authors:Anqi Li, Kai Liu, Linghe Kong, Yulun Zhang
Date:2026-11-20 14:03:18

Latent world models plan by rolling out hundreds of candidate action sequences through a transformer predictor, so a single decision can take tens of seconds. Existing accelerations prune spatial tokens as in vision-language models, yet a sampling-based planner uses predicted costs only to rank candidates, so a rollout can be compressed freely as long as the ranking survives. We propose SOAR, a training-free method that adapts rollout compression to each planning decision. At every decision, SOAR probes a few candidates under several token budgets and keeps the smallest budget that preserves their ranking, so it compresses as far as the current decision allows, without task labels. A closed-loop study of more than 80,000 episodes fixes the rest of the rollout: the first step, which alone reads a real observation, stays exact, and the context frame is cached rather than recomputed. On seven navigation and manipulation tasks with frozen checkpoints, SOAR matches dense-level planning success at 2.3x speedup and retains 98% at 2.5x. Our code is available at https://github.com/starrywiki/SOAR.

Where Delay Compensation Belongs in a Decentralized Mean-Field-Game Swarm with a Control-Barrier Safety Filter

Authors:Jan Mikołajczyk, Igor Rostkowski, Bartosz Ogórkiewicz, Szymon Skierski, Sylwia Opat, Wojciech Rybak, Mikołaj Sczogiel, Radosław Pałys, Jan Gogulski, Bartosz Kościołek, Andrzej Pijanowski, Szymon Kowalski, Marcin Misiewicz
Date:2026-11-20 12:36:13

Large robot teams are often run as a plan-then-execute stack. We study a decentralized instance for drone swarms, a mean-field-game (MFG) routing field with a per-agent control-barrier-function (CBF) safety filter, when communication is limited by a shared TDMA channel. Neighbors are known only through noisy range and bearing measurements exchanged in round-robin slots, so the frame, and with it the age of every estimate, grows with swarm size. In simulation this delay reaches both layers but breaks only the safety layer. Without delay, swarms of up to 100 agents do not collide, even with each measurement's noise held for a whole frame; with it, stale estimates at the CBF cause collisions beyond a frame period of 125 to 175 ms, or 75 to 100 ms at 100 agents with held noise, and swarm size sets where that limit lies and how steeply collisions rise. At the MFG, staleness makes the local density noisier without biasing it, and throughput drops by at most a tenth. We then compensate with lightweight on-board predictors, from constant-velocity fits to a small learned network, that estimate each neighbor's position from its last broadcasts. A layer-split experiment shows where prediction is needed: at the CBF it restores safety and extends the tolerated frame period by 1.4 to 1.7 times with an untrained constant-velocity fit and about 2.3 with the learned network when the noise is redrawn each step; with noise held for a frame the two extend it about equally, while at the MFG it only refines the congestion correction. A constant wider safety margin, the simplest alternative, makes collisions more frequent in a bottleneck. With the routing solved offline, that layer is robust to the delay because it sees neighbors only through a crowd average; compensation belongs in the safety filter, switched on only above the uncompensated limit, since below it the predictor's own error adds collisions.

Date:2026-11-20 12:29:14

Teachers who use generative AI must decide which parts of their work to hand over, while they remain accountable for what reaches students. We studied these decisions in Punjab, Pakistan, through a qualitatively driven mixed-methods study. We interviewed 13 teachers from government, private, NGO and vocational institutions, four administrators and one student, and coded each teacher's position on twelve educational tasks. Nine teachers accepted AI drafts or substantial assistance with lesson planning, but none accepted AI-drafted learning objectives, and eleven did not use AI to encourage or emotionally support students. Teachers who would accept AI marking required that the model be given a rubric or marking criteria. Date:2026-11-20 04:49:56

If we optimize an image until a classifier is confident it shows a cat, we may get an adversarial example, not a cat. Planning with a latent world model is a similar search for actions whose predicted future is closest to the goal. Is the result a plan, or an adversarial example of the world model? We show that it can be either. On the released DINO-WM and LeWM, more iterations of the cross-entropy method (CEM) lower the predicted cost but lose up to half of the problems CEM had already solved.
