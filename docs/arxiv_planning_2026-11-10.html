Certified Zonotopic Reachability Analysis for Trajectory Tracking on Lie Groups

Authors:Yanliang Huang, Peng Xie, Zhen Zhang, Zhuoqi Zeng, Amr Alanwar
Date:2026-11-09 17:40:43

Autonomous vehicles track planned trajectories close to obstacles. Unknown disturbances make them deviate from these trajectories. With a bound on the tracking error that disturbances can cause, a planned trajectory can be certified as safe before execution. Existing set-based methods bound the tracking error by one invariant ellipsoid for the whole trajectory or by intervals, which are both conservative. For aerial and space vehicles, the tracking error evolves on a matrix Lie group with nonlinear dynamics. However, for group-affine systems, the error dynamics are linear in Lie algebra coordinates when the vehicle receives the inputs of the reference. The control input and the disturbance enter through the inverse right Jacobian, a matrix that depends on the error. We propagate the linear part of the error dynamics with zonotopes and enclose the effect of the inverse right Jacobian on the disturbance in closed form. The resulting tubes are tight and provably contain all reachable tracking errors in continuous time. When some error components have no input, the inverse right Jacobian leaves unmatched nonlinear terms in the closed loop. None of the tested methods certifies such a closed loop. Hybrid coordinates make these terms matched, so that our controller cancels every nonlinear term that does not contain the disturbance. Our tubes give tighter bounds than the ellipsoid method in simulations of a planar aircraft and a fully actuated multirotor and in real flights of a quadrotor, where the tube contains every measured attitude error. Under larger disturbances and the same feedback gain, the ellipsoid method yields no valid ellipsoid, whereas our tubes remain valid.

Across synthetic dynamical systems and robot locomotion benchmarks, TabFM is more accurate than LSTM and MLP models trained from scratch on the same limited transitions in almost all settings, and among regressors fitted to the same data it is the most accurate on locomotion, while a Gaussian process is more accurate on smooth low-dimensional systems. However, models trained on the full dataset remain substantially better on most systems. When rolled out autoregressively over multiple steps, TabFM's accuracy depends strongly on how much context it is given. With a short context, its rollouts quickly degrade to the level of a trivial baseline, whereas with a long context they improve substantially and, on one locomotion task, exceed a trained LSTM. Inference is also about three orders of magnitude slower than an LSTM. Overall, in their current state, we find that training-free priors are useful as predictors when data are scarce and enough context is available, but they are not yet a substitute for trained world models in planning.

How Many Candidates Does a One-Step World Model Need? A Random-Codebook Account of Nearest-of-m Training on Frozen DINO Tokens

Authors:Yong Hwi Song, Keon Myung Lee
Date:2026-11-09 05:09:42

One-step stochastic world models avoid the mode averaging of deterministic feature predictors without the tens of network evaluations per step that diffusion and flow matching require. The simplest such objective is nearest-of-m matching (conditional implicit maximum likelihood estimation, IMLE): draw m candidate futures and pull only the one closest to the observed outcome. Recent work conjectures that a small m suffices for feature-space world models and that a strictly proper energy score should replace the hard minimum. We test both claims with one-step world models on frozen DINOv2/v3 patch tokens, using benchmarks whose futures can be enumerated. Under mode-like uncertainty, nearest-of-m with m=16 covers the enumerated futures better than 50-step flow matching (at about 1/30 of its latency), one-step flow generators and an energy-score generator at matched parameter counts, and its rollouts stay on the token manifold where flow rollouts drift. One quantity governs the choice of m: the nearest-of-m objective is the expected distortion of a random codebook of m draws from the generator. This view yields three predictions, recorded before the corresponding runs: (i) memorization of the realized outcome in contexts that never repeat, whose strength is governed by the codebook's miss distortion I(m) rather than its miss probability, and which an energy-score generator does not escape by raising m; (ii) a flattening of mode probabilities with m, matching a known optimum within 0.01; and (iii) under-dispersion on continuous high-dimensional outcomes, which bounds the method on real robot video. Per-step matching on self-rollouts cuts long-horizon error by a third on a stochastic Push-T variant; an ablation separates its manifold and coverage benefits. Planning success does not improve despite accurate risk estimates, a negative result we report with its diagnosis.

Tropical Gaussian Anticoncentration: Optimal Instance-Dependent Bounds for Online Learning in Extensive-Form Games

Authors:Ashkan Soleymani, Zhiyuan Fan, Lillian J. Ratliff, Patrick Jaillet, Gabriele Farina
Date:2026-11-09 04:54:21

We study the minimax regret of full-information online decision making in extensive-form games. For prediction with expert advice, the optimal regret is $Θ(\sqrt{T\log K})$, where $K$ is the number of experts, or equivalently, the number of normal-form actions. For treeplex strategy spaces, existing efficient algorithms, including KOMWU and online mirror descent with the weight-one dilated entropy regularizer, achieve regret $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T\log|\mathcal{V}|})$, where $\mathcal{V}$ is the set of reduced normal-form strategies. We prove that this logarithmic dependence is minimax optimal in the worst-case full-information reward model. The main difficulty is that pure plans in a treeplex are not independent experts: their rewards are correlated through the recursive structure of decisions and observations. To capture this correlation, we introduce tropical Gaussian formulae, recursive Gaussian expressions built from maxima and normalized sums which preserve the variance. The index count of such a formula matches the number of reduced normal-form continuation plans in the corresponding subtree. Our main analytic result shows that every log-balanced tropical Gaussian formula with index count $N$ has Gaussian width $Ω(\sqrt{\log N})$. While a direct approach struggles to recover this lower bound, our proof uses a spherical entropy profile to measure scale-dependent separation among the Gaussian directions, and then applies Turán's theorem and Sudakov minoration to obtain the width lower bound. Applying this result to treeplexes gives an oblivious hard distribution with valid transition outcome and Rademacher terminal rewards, establishing a regret lower bound of $Ω(\sqrt{T\log|\mathcal{V}|})$. This matches known upper bounds up to universal constants and shows that the DilEnt/KOMWU dependence on the reduced normal-form complexity is unavoidable.

Action Representation for Spatial Generalization in Robot World Models

Authors:Yuhang Sun
Date:2026-11-09 04:45:09

Spatial generalization in robot world models depends on whether action representations capture the physical relationships that remain valid across changing spatial configurations. Yet different coordinates can describe the same interaction, whereas similar motions can produce different outcomes when local physical conditions change. How these distinctions affect learned prediction remains insufficiently understood. This study evaluates matched action representations defined in world, object, and surface coordinate frames using a controlled robotic peeling benchmark. Global pose and contact relocation experiments reveal a cost associated with limited training data when equivalent interactions receive different spatial descriptions. Removing information about peel thickness creates an irreducible ambiguity because identical predictive inputs can correspond to different material removal outcomes. Coordinates defined relative to the surface improve consequence prediction and counterfactual action selection under accurate local observations, but learned predictors remain vulnerable to uncertainty in the coordinate frame. Joint centering of the surface and tool removes sensitivity to translation of their common origin, whereas incorrect contact geometry and actual execution deviations introduce different failure mechanisms. These results show that effective spatial generalization depends on preserving the relationships between actions and states that determine physical consequences, rather than simply minimizing coordinate variation. This perspective provides a principled basis for designing robot world models that reuse physical knowledge across spatial configurations while retaining the distinctions essential to prediction and planning.

Dense maps of room acoustic parameters (T30, EDT, C50, DRR) describe how a room sounds at every position. Scene-wide estimation predicts such maps from a floor plan but requires a reference impulse response, which is hard to obtain. Our system instead receives a floor plan and easily recorded reverberant speech from one talker-microphone pair. We compare four speech-derived conditioning inputs: MFCCs, an embedding from the blind estimator BERP, BERP's scalar reverberation-time estimate, and an impulse response reconstructed by Rec-RIR. Our main benchmark is the MRAS dataset, where material assignments vary at fixed geometry. ROS2MR: Connecting ROS 2 Motion Planning to Mixed-Reality Trajectory Visualization

Authors:Santiago Berrezueta-Guzman, Habiba-Loai Khalil, Andrei Koshelev, Stefan Wagner
Date:2026-11-09 03:48:12

Mixed reality (MR) allows users to inspect planned robot motion within their physical surroundings, but displaying trajectories from robotics software requires converting their joint configurations and timing into a format the MR application can play back. This paper presents ROS2MR, an extension of the MR-Robotics LAB platform that converts YAML-serialized ROS 2 joint trajectories, including MoveIt motion plans, into the platform's existing JSON playback format. The converter validates the input, maps arm joints by name, and resamples the trajectory at a nominal rate of 30 Hz, using cubic Hermite interpolation when joint velocities are supplied and linear interpolation otherwise, while binary gripper commands are sampled separately using a zero-order hold. A Python export helper combines sequences of motion plans and gripper states into a single trajectory and checks positional agreement at segment boundaries. The MR application was also adapted for Apple Vision Pro in addition to Meta Quest 3. A technical evaluation showed that the converter preserved the normalized duration and endpoint configurations for dense, sparse, and irregularly timed inputs. With supplied velocities, the position RMSE was approximately 2.91x10^-5 degrees for all sampling patterns; without them, sparse input increased it to 0.85 degrees, and close positional agreement did not guarantee accurate velocity profiles. A pick-and-place sequence retained its open--closed--open gripper order, although off-grid transitions were shifted to the output sampling grid. The resulting file-based pathway enables inspection of ROS-generated trajectories in MR without a live ROS connection.
