Date:2026-10-15 17:39:16

When robots are deployed in the real-world, they must interact with other agents and accomplish their own goals while adhering to legal constraints. These potentially conflicting objectives are often only partially prioritized, making scalarized objectives or fully specified hierarchies unsuitable. We present a framework for multi-agent motion planning under legal requirements based on games with partially ordered preferences (posetal games). Scenario-independent legal guidelines order abstract legal concerns, from which we automatically derive agent preferences and a legal game for any concrete scenario. Agents may refine their legal preferences with private objectives, and regulators may refine the guideline as laws evolve. Explore Once, Evaluate Many: Multiple Policy Evaluation for Stochastic Linear Bandits

Authors:Spyros Dragazis, Yilei Chen, Aldo Pacchiano
Date:2026-10-15 17:22:59

We study how to evaluate $K$ target policies simultaneously in stochastic contextual bandits with linear reward structure. Given a known $d$-dimensional feature map and reward-free access to policy queries, we propose PRISMA (Policy evaluation through Regularized Information Sharing and Mixture Allocation), an algorithm that estimates every target policy's value to accuracy $\varepsilon$ with probability at least $1-δ$. The algorithm uses $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\!\left( \frac{(L^2S^2+β^2)(1\veeρ^\star)}{\varepsilon^2} \cdot \log(K) \right)$ context draws and reward observations, where $ρ^\star\le d$ is an instance-dependent measure of the difficulty of designing a shared sampling distribution. Here, $L$, $S$, and $β$ bound the feature norm, reward parameter norm, and reward noise, respectively, and the suppressed factors are logarithmic in $K$, $d$, and $1/δ$. Our method combines reward-free planning, a regularized sampling design, and a single ridge regression estimator shared across all target policies. An adaptive doubling procedure selects the regularization scale without knowledge of $ρ^\star$. The guarantees require no numerical lower bound on second-moment eigenvalues and have no explicit dependence on the cardinalities of the context and action spaces. Policy-query costs are accounted for separately, with $K$ queries per shared planning context.
