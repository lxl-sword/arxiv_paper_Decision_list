Date:2026-10-16 17:58:51

Mobile health (mHealth) apps collect personally-identifiable information while handling uniquely sensitive medical data, ranging from mental health records to real-time glucose or heart rate monitoring -- yet they remain poorly understood at scale. We present the first comprehensive analysis of consent and data collection practices across 10,000+ mHealth apps on the Google Play Store, combining static analysis and privacy policy examination. We develop a rigorous operational definition to distinguish health apps from general wellness programs, and design a measurement framework to detect misuse of permissions and violations of consent. Our analyses reveal a troubling ecosystem: 69.15% of apps request at least one permission unrelated to their stated purpose, with over 52.9% apps requesting location data, and 50.5% and 34.9% apps requesting camera and microphone access, respectively -- despite no functional justification based on app descriptions and stated purposes. We plan to release our dataset and measurement tools for continued monitoring of this critical space.

Jarvis: Coordination Deserves a Brain of Its Own in Agentic Interactive Models

Date:2026-10-16 17:29:10

Interactive models increasingly hand complex reasoning and tool use to a slow brain in the background, leaving a realtime front model free to sustain full-duplex dialogue. Yet the user may refine, cancel or replace a request while its task runs, so a plan or result that made sense a moment ago goes stale. Each new utterance must therefore be read against the work in flight, but the front model follows only the conversation and the back end sees only its own task. Coordination deserves a brain of its own, one that sees both. We introduce Jarvis, a coordination harness where models judge and code enforces. Its typed decision model, JEV, reads each turn against the live tasks and picks among operations built from a versioned task record. With the front model and back end held fixed, replacing Qwen Audio Agent with Jarvis raises tau-Voice task success more than sixfold, from 29 to 192 of 278 tasks, and the gains hold on every benchmark and front model. Against published systems, Jarvis-Audio reaches 69% Pass1 on Full-Duplex-Bench v3 with an open 9B front model, versus 60% for GPT-Realtime. These results suggest that how the two brains are coordinated matters as much as how strong they are.

Beyond Action Prediction: Learning Search-Free Planning in Latent Space

Authors:Guanchen Du, Jinhao Du, Yanpeng Sun, Shan Zhang, Na Zhao, Zechao Li
Date:2026-10-16 15:26:24

Latent world models learn action-conditioned state transitions in a compact representation space, providing a predictive model for visual planning and control. However, planning with these models typically requires costly test-time optimization over action sequences. We propose to replace this online search with a goal-conditioned inverse model that directly predicts actions from current and goal observations. A key challenge is that action prediction alone neither ensures that goal representations encode action-relevant information nor that predicted actions are consistent with the learned latent prediction model. We introduce Hindsight Inverse JEPA Planning (HIJP), a search-free framework that addresses both aspects during training. HIJP relabels future observations along offline trajectories as goals, providing action supervision across multiple temporal horizons. Across four visual control tasks, HIJP achieves 86.8% overall success and a 1.9-16.5x planning speedup over search-based planning.

Beyond Expected Values: Risk-Sensitive Planning with Distributional Monte-Carlo Tree Search

Authors:Quynh Trinh, Trung Nguyen, Nam Nguyen, Tuan Dam
Date:2026-10-16 14:51:28

Monte-Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) typically stores only scalar mean estimates, which makes it poorly suited for tail-risk objectives such as conditional value-at-risk (CVaR). We introduce Categorical Thompson Sampling with Optimism (CATSO) and Particle Thompson Sampling with Optimism (PATSO), two distributional MCTS algorithms that maintain finite-support empirical backup laws at Q-edges. CATSO represents Q-edge laws with categorical atoms and Dirichlet Thompson sampling, while PATSO represents them with adaptive particles. Both algorithms select actions using the CVaR of a Thompson-sampled Q-edge law plus a polynomial optimism bonus, and propagate scalar continuation values through visit-weighted averages of child Q-edge CVaR scores. CATSO adds a fixed-grid discretization bias, while capped PATSO adds a tunable Wasserstein compression bias. Experiments on stochastic planning benchmarks show that distributional Q-edges improve lower-tail return and reduce catastrophic outcomes when mean-optimal and risk-sensitive behavior differ.

Authors:Kleitos Papadopoulos
Date:2026-10-16 10:26:16

We study deterministic single-item lot sizing with all-units quantity discounts, linear holding costs, and time-varying end-of-period inventory bounds. Unit prices may vary arbitrarily over time. For one common positive discount threshold, we give an exact algorithm requiring $O(n^3)$ arithmetic operations and $O(n^2)$ working space, including recovery of an optimal purchase plan. The method combines a critical-state theorem in cumulative-purchase coordinates with two prefix-minimum scans per dynamic-programming layer. The structural argument accommodates downward price discontinuities and distinguishes prescribed from free terminal inventory. With a fixed global set of $m$ positive threshold values, the bounds become $O_m(n^{m+2})$ time and $O_m(n^{m+1})$ space. Period-specific thresholds admit the same exact construction, but its state set can be exponential. For commensurate flow data, an inventory-lattice implementation takes $O(PW)$ time, where $P$ is the total number of price tiers and $W$ is the largest inventory-lattice width. We also derive a conditional optimality certificate from a monotone one-threshold relaxation; this certificate does not imply a near-linear worst-case algorithm for arbitrary prices. Exact-arithmetic implementations are checked against exhaustive inventory dynamic programs and tier-enumeration linear programs. An illustrative timing study identifies settings in which the lattice implementation is preferable.
