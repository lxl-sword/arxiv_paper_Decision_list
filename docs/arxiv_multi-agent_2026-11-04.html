Safeguarding Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Connected and Automated Vehicles with Recursively Feasible Control Barrier Functions

Authors:Jianye Xu, Yousef Sayed, Bassam Alrifaee
Date:2026-11-03 14:16:51

Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) shows promise in coordinating connected and automated vehicles but offers no safety guarantee. Although Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) can add this guarantee, their optimization problem can become infeasible in practice due to control bounds. Moreover, they often overapproximate vehicle and road geometry, which makes them conservative. This paper proposes the Backup-Horizon CBF (BH-CBF), which evaluates safety along a horizon until all vehicles stop. BH-CBF is recursively feasible under control bounds, and it considers the exact vehicle and road geometry. Based on BH-CBF, we derive a fully decentralized safety filter that only needs a single broadcast of vehicle states and routes per step and stays real-time in dense traffic. We use BH-CBF to safeguard a MARL policy over its life cycle: as a reward during pre-training, as a run-time filter at deployment, and as a filter in the training loop that keeps fine-tuning on unseen maps collision-free. In simulations on real-world maps of a roundabout and an intersection, BH-CBF avoids all collisions of unsafe MARL policies and keeps its safety guarantee at every step. Existing filters either collide, lose the guarantee, or fall back to full braking far more often. In recorded roundabout traffic with 1264 human-driven vehicles over 115 km, our safeguarded policies shorten the median travel time by 14% compared with the human drivers, without any collision.

Finite-Sample Convergence in Networked Average Reward MARL: Decentralization Pitfalls and Entropy Remedies

Authors:Yizhou Zhang, Yashaswini Murthy, Laixi Shi, Adam Vierman
Date:2026-11-02 23:34:29

We study the problem of average reward Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) where agents interact within a network. Each agent in the network conducts local updates with information within its $k$-hop neighborhood and collaboratively maximizes the overall reward of the entire network. We first provide impossibility results in achieving convergence to the global optimal joint policies in our setting. These impossibility results highlight the challenges of decentralized learning with local information in multi-agent systems, namely the issues of multiagency, where each agent independently optimizes its policy, and partial observability, where agents have limited visibility into the global state. Given these challenges which indicate that decentralized policy optimization can generally be certified only up to stationarity, we provide finite-sample convergence guarantees for a decentralized actor-critic algorithm with linear function approximation that converges to an approximate stationary point with small gradient norm. To overcome such impossibility results, we further show that adding fixed-state entropy regularization reshapes the objective landscape which leads to even stronger global convergence guarantees for our proposed actor-critic algorithm.

Our focus is on long-horizon, centrally monitored cooperative systems such as warehouse robotics, automated storage-and-retrieval systems, and cloud-resource scheduling, where teams share a reward, operate under shifting objectives, and already run with global telemetry at deployment. This is sufficient when agents are weakly coupled or the environment is stationary, but it fails when the team must realign after conditions change. The limitation is structural rather than algorithmic, and more training cannot add a channel that does not exist at execution time. We call this the recoverability gap. To address it, we name the underexplored design space of intermittent test-time coordination as centralized training with mixed execution (CTME), in which a coordinator observes global state and emits high-level signals while agents act locally. CTME is not a particular learning algorithm, but a cooperative MARL problem setting that makes test-time information structure explicit. It specifies team-level information, high-level signals, and update frequency after training, with CTDE recovered as a limit case. We do not argue that CTDE is obsolete. We argue that removing the test-time channel is a modeling choice, not a necessity, and the wrong default when global telemetry is available and teams must recover from drift.

Collective Autonomous Vehicle (AV) route choices in real-world traffic networks can create externalities for Human-Driven Vehicles (HDVs), as routes that benefit AVs may increase travel times for other road users. To address this, we introduce a social component, the marginal cost, to the selfish travel time AV reward. Marginal cost captures an AV's impact on other road users by comparing their travel times with those in a counterfactual simulation in which the AV is removed. We study AV route choice in mixed-autonomy traffic settings using Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), where AVs learn routing policies through repeated interactions with the traffic environment. Experiments on the real-world networks of Ingolstadt and Provins show that incorporating marginal cost into the AV reward not only limits the increase in HDV travel times and shortens AV travel times relative to selfish AV routing, but also improves overall system performance. For a more scalable approach to social AV routing in mixed-autonomy traffic, we compute the marginal cost only for the AVs whose route choices most affect the traffic system. This reduces the number of counterfactual simulations and achieves similar performance at a significantly lower computational cost. Our results suggest that AV routing should go beyond selfish rewards and, for the benefit of all traffic participants, include a social component in its objective.
