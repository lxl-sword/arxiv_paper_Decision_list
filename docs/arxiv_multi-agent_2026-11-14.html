A canonical example is a mobility operator that orchestrates a fleet of vehicles to meet customer demand. The stochastic and time-varying nature of these problems calls for learning approaches, yet existing MARL algorithms do not scale: learning assignment and routing jointly over the full state-action space becomes prohibitive as the number of agents grows. Formulating the problem over the space of probability measures, we prove a separation principle: the population cost-to-go function is upper bounded by an optimal transport problem whose transportation cost is the target-conditioned single-agent cost-to-go function. In a vehicle-delivery experiment on the southern Manhattan road network, where MARL baselines are computationally prohibitive, SALT improves travel time by 15-21% over a shortest-path routing heuristic. Flexible job-shop scheduling becomes resource-coupled when a scarce vehicle fleet moves jobs between stations, because the schedule sets the transport waits. BOLT (bound-oriented learning for transport-coupled scheduling) builds a lower bound from chain, machine-capacity, and fleet-capacity relaxations. The bound equals the makespan at termination, so its per-step changes form a reward that sums to a constant minus the makespan. Because it never exceeds the attainable makespan, each candidate's bound increase is a proven rise of the makespan floor, which the policy reads as a price. The root bound certifies a completed schedule's suboptimality gap without a solver. In cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), agents must learn to coordinate their actions to achieve shared objectives. A key indicator of effective coordination is behavioral diversity, i.e., the agents' potential to enact distinct yet complementary control policies given the same set of observations. Although behavioral diversity can enable role specialization and richer joint behaviors, its appropriate level is highly context- and task-dependent and often costly to tune. We propose Adaptive Diversity Control (ADiCo), a two-timescale framework that adapts behavioral diversity online during training. On a fast timescale, Diversity Control (DiCo) regulates system diversity toward a target value. On a slower timescale, an Extremum Seeking Controller (ESC) adjusts this target using only mean episodic returns, without requiring an explicit model or analytical gradient of the diversity-performance landscape. Through this adaptation, ADiCo matches or exceeds the mean performance of DiCo (best grid-searched diversity target) and Unconstrained DiCo (diversity allowed to emerge without target regulation). These results demonstrate that incorporating classical feedback control into MARL training enables effective coordination by providing a systematic alternative to task-specific manual selection of the diversity target.
