Disturbance-Aware Informative Planning in Spatiotemporal Environments

Authors:Kaleb Ben Naveed, Haejoon Lee, Anouck Girard, Dimitra Panagou
Date:2026-11-13 18:22:50

Autonomous robots are widely used for persistent monitoring of spatiotemporal environments, where quantities of interest (QoI) change across space and time and require repeated measurements. In practice, robot motion can be affected by external disturbances that vary across the environment. When such external disturbances are not considered in trajectory optimization, it can lead to inaccurate predictions of robot motion and, consequently, the information acquired about the QoI during execution. However, estimating disturbances throughout the environment can be inefficient, as disturbances in regions with little overlap with monitoring trajectories have limited impact on monitoring performance. We therefore propose a two-stage informative planning framework for unknown, spatially varying but time-invariant disturbances, combining informed disturbance sampling with disturbance-aware trajectory optimization. First, we identify where QoI measurements are needed and generate alternative paths to these locations using the environment geometry. We then prioritize disturbance measurements based on their expected reduction of disturbance uncertainty along these paths. Second, rather than optimizing information acquisition based on nominal robot motion, we incorporate the learned disturbance belief into trajectory optimization to account for its effects on robot motion and information acquisition. Across four simulation scenarios, the proposed method reduces disturbance-learning time by 25.0--52.3 percent compared with environment-wide estimation and identifies paths that appear costly under nominal dynamics but become preferable when disturbances are considered.

Its consumer-credit collections generator provides five partially observed environments that share known dynamics matrices and vary a memory parameter and process noise. The experiments change selected inputs or planning rules while keeping the rest of the solver fixed. Each comparison specifies the solver's state inputs, response inputs and planning horizon. CT-SGAL-LIO: Causal Continuous Time Refinement of Discrete LiDAR Inertial Odometry

Authors:Nam Tran, Thu Tran, Toan Nguyen, William J Beksi, Tuan Dang
Date:2026-11-12 15:07:17

State-of-the-art LiDAR-inertial odometry methods, including those on richer state manifolds, achieve strong global accuracy, yet their pose stream alternates between propagation and correction and can oscillate over short horizons even when globally accurate. Most existing methods follow these approaches: they either modify the filter to enable faster updates or estimate a continuous-time trajectory from raw LiDAR and inertial data. The latter involves extra optimization, delaying state updates and becoming less robust during rapid rotations or accelerations. The key novelty is to perform continuous-time trajectory refinement directly on the projected SGal(3) posterior: we fit a cubic trajectory over a bounded window of recent poses and evaluate it at a small fixed lag. Across 17 sequences from GRAND-TOUR, MCD, and GEODE, CT-SGAL-LIO improves short-term motion accuracy while largely preserving global accuracy. RTE improves by up to 70% on GRAND-TOUR and on all seven GEODE sequences, with 9.9-51.1% reductions. A 19.5-min R-Campus run also reduces posterior jumps by 85.4% with only a 4.7 mm change in the end-to-start gap. We plan to release our source code and results to support community validation and reproducibility.
