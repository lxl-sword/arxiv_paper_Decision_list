Learning Visuomotor Quadrotor Flight Through Dynamic Narrow Gaps via Differentiable Simulation

Authors:Riccardo Bianco, Yunfan Ren, Jiaxu Xing, Davide Scaramuzza
Date:2026-10-27 17:05:05

Threading a quadrotor through a moving narrow gap is a demanding test of agile flight, requiring centimeter-level spatial alignment and precise temporal execution. We address this challenge using a control policy that operates directly on onboard visual and inertial measurements to output collective thrust and body-rate commands. The task is difficult because success lies on a thin feasible manifold: small errors in timing, position, attitude, or visual observability cause collision. Prior planning and learning methods rely on privileged state, teachers, planners, or distillation; learning dynamic narrow-gap traversal directly, from scratch, remains open. We introduce a differentiable moving-gap formulation that gates centering, alignment, and smooth collision terms by signed distance to a time-varying gap plane, turning a sparse traversal event into a dense pathwise objective. We integrate this formulation into a visual-feature-to-command policy trained in one stage from random initialization, without a teacher, planner, or distillation. We validate our approach in both synthetic and real-world experiments. In controlled benchmarks, we demonstrate that pathwise gradients significantly outperform score-function gradients for precision-critical tasks, yielding a 99.3% success rate in simulation after only a few minutes of training on a single GPU. Furthermore, the policy transfers directly to a real quadrotor, successfully crossing a dynamic narrow gap from varied initial conditions using onboard sensing and inertial measurements, achieving an 80% success rate over 10 physical trials.

Conformal prediction calibrates a keepout around the mapped walls that covers the true unsafe set at a chosen probability. Typically an unmodified planner treats the keepout as an inflated obstacle, and nothing ensures that the vehicle can stop before the keepout as it moves when sensing degrades. Date:2026-10-26 23:48:18

Generative AI can produce code, tests, documentation, and technical explanations that resemble artefacts traditionally used as evidence of student competence, weakening the inference from artefact quality to the engineering capability behind it. Bayesian Replicator Dynamics and the Inversion of Strict Dominance in Bimatrix Games

Authors:Jaime Ruiz-Serra, Nihat Ay, Michael Harré
Date:2026-10-26 22:11:38

Replicator dynamics, the central model of evolutionary game theory, describe populations evolving under selection and agents learning by trial and error. They also admit a third use: continuous-time Bayesian inference, in which an agent planning against an opponent accumulates evidence for each candidate strategy. Here we show that this use divides all two-player games into two classes. With the standard expected-utility payoff, the dynamics can be expressed as Bayesian updating only in games (with any number of actions) where no player has a dominant strategy. The logarithmic payoff of Ay and Erb admits the Bayesian representation in every game. Coordination, anti-coordination, and cyclic games share qualitative dynamics under both payoff definitions, with equilibria displaced and selection thresholds shifted. In contrast, the flows of every game with a dominant strategy are reversed between the payoff definitions, with the strictly dominant strategy eliminated under logarithmic payoffs. Cooperation therefore emerges in the Prisoner's Dilemma, and games of common interest converge to mutual defection. For two-action games the classification is complete and rests on an exact Hamiltonian structure shared by both payoff definitions. The structure gives a behavioural test: cycles in the classic Matching Pennies game have amplitude-independent period (isochronous) under logarithmic payoffs, but not under expected utility. Logarithmic evidence integration thus determines which strategic outcomes dynamical selection can reach, wherever learning approximates Bayesian updating.
