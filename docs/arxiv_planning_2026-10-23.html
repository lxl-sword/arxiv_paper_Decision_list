Extending Harmonic Function Methods via Root Locus for Coordinated Path Generation

Authors:Matteo Frascarelli, Lucia Pallottino, Alberto Landi
Date:2026-10-22 17:45:31

We consider the offline generation of coordinated paths for N interchangeable robots and N goals in planar environments with polygonal obstacles. After a conformal map to a circular domain, starts and goals become the poles and zeros of a rational function R, and the paths are the branches of its Generalized Root Locus (GRL), i.e. the streamlines of the logarithmic potential -log R. We show that, for every phase outside a finite set of critical phases and a set of boundary phases, the induced gradient motion drives each robot to a distinct goal along pairwise disjoint paths that never reach the obstacles; the robot-goal assignment is produced by the construction, and with the gain used as a clock all robots move as the closed-loop roots of the GRL and arrive together at a prescribed time. Obstacles become streamlines through successive Milne-Thomson reflections, and for a finite number of reflections a finite test certifies safety for the potential that is actually computed. Reciprocal root-locus planning is recovered as a special case. The method is illustrated on two polygonal workspaces, 100 random instances compared with independent harmonic planning and artificial potentials, and a scene with ten robots and ten obstacles.

Active inference casts perception, learning and planning as variational inference, where the Expected Free Energy (EFE) defines the planning objective. Recent work showed that EFE minimization can be written as Variational Free Energy (VFE) minimization on a generative model augmented with epistemic priors, and that the resulting entropy-corrected objective is minimized by sum-product message passing (SP-MP) on modified factor functions. These formulations treat model uncertainty as inference over a finite set of known models. We extend them to planning during learning, where beliefs over the observation and transition parameters $θ_A$, $θ_B$ are Dirichlet priors. Closed-form Dirichlet updates are usually obtained by a structured mean-field factorization between parameters and states, but under exactly that factorization the information-gain corrections vanish. Representation learning has substantially improved the efficiency of unconstrained continuous-state Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), but its extension to Constrained MDPs (CMDPs) is difficult because the transition representation must be learned while constraint violation is controlled. In this paper, we study representation learning for low-rank CMDPs with continuous state spaces, and propose REP-PD-TEG for the soft cumulative-violation setting. In the feature learning period, the algorithm interacts with the environment using a decaying-$ε$-greedy mixture execution policy, and uses the collected samples to learn transition features by maximum likelihood. Based on the learned features, it constructs uncertainty bonuses and plans with an optimistic Lagrangian objective to obtain the return policy. It plans under a bonus-corrected lower-confidence utility certificate with the learned representation, and performs a stabilized dual search to obtain the return policy, yielding regret and hard-violation bounds. Together, these results give the first theoretically guaranteed representation-learning method for low-rank continuous-state CMDPs with both soft cumulative guarantees and certified hard-violation control.

Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSSs) are evolving from closed navigation constellations toward open space infrastructure capable of serving extensive spacecraft users. This evolution requires the constellation to support navigation ranging, telemetry (TM) return, and telecommand (TC) delivery for users while preserving its own ranging and communication functions.Existing phased-array payloads onboard BeiDou satellites provide one transmit (Tx) beam and one receive (Rx) beam. Upgrading GNSS satellites to phased-array terminals with $x$-Tx and $y$-Rx beams is therefore a natural enhancement. The results further show that additional Tx and Rx beams can be effectively converted into multi-service capability. This work establishes a link-scheduling approach for extending GNSS from constellation-internal operation to large-scale spacecraft service support.

We propose AgentDreamer, an agent-guided optimization framework that aligns text instructions with simulated motion through explicit 3D motion planning. Given a text instruction and scene geometry, an off-the-shelf agent translates the instruction into a structured semantic motion prior, specifying what should move, in which direction, and in what temporal order. The agent represents this prior using parametric functions over a series of temporal chunks, yielding dense 3D trajectories. We then optimize the parameters of a differentiable MPM simulator such that its rollout best matches the prior while remaining constrained by the scene dynamics. This allows the agent to provide semantic guidance from language, while the simulator grounds it in physically feasible motion. We further introduce Motion-IG, an information-theoretic metric for text-motion alignment based on masked text recovery. By comparing the likelihood of recovering masked motion-related content with and without the simulated motion, Motion-IG measures the information contributed by the motion beyond that available from the remaining text and scene context. Experiments show that AgentDreamer improves text alignment over video-based guidance, producing simulations that more closely follow the requested physical behavior.

Continuous-Time Motion Planning and Steering with Global Convergence

Authors:Mohsen Amiri, Sima Didari, Davoud Ataee Tarzanagh, Hankyu Moon, Yeongdae Kwon
Date:2026-10-22 02:15:39

Motion planning is central to robotics, and it is increasingly used to correct the motions proposed by learned planners and policies. In both uses, constraints must hold at every instant of the motion, not only at sampled times. To our knowledge, no algorithm provably converges to a globally optimal motion once a smoothness cost is combined with such constraints. We cast planning and steering as a single continuous-time program: robots avoid one another and static or moving obstacles, and a learned proposal is steered by keeping the motion close to it. The program is elementary to state, yet nonconvex. We propose GlobMP (Global Motion Planning), a gradient projection method over piecewise-polynomial curves, on which each constraint is one polynomial per piece that must stay nonnegative over the whole motion; it alternates cost reduction with feasibility restoration. Under mild assumptions, we show that, from a fine grid start, its cost approaches the global optimum as the number of polynomial pieces grows, with every constraint met to any chosen tolerance, or exactly on stable instances. We demonstrate its superior performance over strong baselines in real and synthetic experiments, including teams of up to $200$ robots, multi-robot planning on the Robotarium testbed and an extended benchmark, maze navigation, and steering tactile-reactive policies on a bimanual humanoid.
